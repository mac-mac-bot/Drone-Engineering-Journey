# My Remote Pilot Training Journey 🚁

I have completed 5 days of remote pilot training.
This document covers the progress of my learning, practical experience, and concepts learned throughout the course.

---

# Summary of Training

* **Day 1** – Drone regulations, flying conditions, drone categories, and flight fundamentals.
* **Day 2** – D-Forms, drone components, software usage, payloads, atmosphere, and safety checks.
* **Day 3** – Drone simulation training using a controller and learning drone movements.
* **Day 4** – Practical flying exercises including straight lines, squares, and half circles.
* **Day 5** – Advanced practical flying including full circles and figure-8 patterns with confidence.

---

# Day 1

On the first day, I learned the fundamentals of drones and drone operations.

Topics covered:

* Stakeholders in the drone ecosystem
* Categories of drones
* DGCA regulations
* D-Forms
* Drone flying zones:

  * Red Zone
  * Yellow Zone
  * Green Zone
* Flight modes:

  * Position Mode
  * Altitude Mode
  * Manual Mode
  * Aero Mode

I also learned the fundamentals of drone aerodynamics, including:

* Airfoil
* Chord line
* Fundamental forces acting on a drone

This helped me understand how drones generate lift and maintain stability during flight.

---

# Day 2

On the second day, I learned about drone hardware, communication procedures, atmosphere layers, and safety assessments.

Topics covered:

* Components of drones
* Mission Planner software
* Standard communication messages:

  * STANDBY
  * AFFIRM
  * NEGATIVE
* Payloads and their types

I also learned about atmospheric layers:

* Troposphere
* Stratosphere
* Mesosphere

Environmental properties studied:

* Pressure
* Temperature
* Humidity
* Density
* Mean Sea Level (MSL)
* Lapse Rate

Safety and operational topics:

* Risk assessment
* Pre-flight checks
* Post-flight checks

This day helped me understand how environmental conditions affect drone performance and safety.

---

# Day 3

On the third day, I started practical drone simulation training using a controller connected to a computer.

I learned:

* Yaw
* Roll
* Pitch
* Throttle

I also learned:

* Proper handling of the controller
* Smooth drone movements
* Position control
* Basic maneuvering

Using the simulator felt similar to a video game, but it helped build coordination and confidence before real drone operation.

I practiced drawing basic flight patterns and shapes using the drone simulator.

---

# Day 4

On the fourth day, I moved to real-world practical drone flying.

I learned:

* How to remove and connect the battery safely
* Proper battery charging methods
* Practical pre-flight and post-flight checks

I also learned about drone safety settings such as:

* Inner boundary limits
* Outer boundary limits

These settings help prevent the drone from going out of control or flying outside the allowed region.

I practiced:

* Straight-line flying
* Half circles
* Squares
* Left and right directional movement
* Proper drone takeoff and landing procedures

Additionally, I learned how to check whether weather conditions are suitable for flying.

---

# Day 5

On the fifth day, I independently performed practical drone flying exercises with improved confidence and control.

I practiced:

* Full circles
* Clockwise and anticlockwise maneuvers
* Figure-8 flight patterns

This was the final stage of my practical flying training, where I was able to confidently control the drone and complete all required maneuvers successfully.

---

# Final Observation

On the final day, weather conditions were not suitable for flying, and the session was cancelled.

This experience helped me understand an important real-world lesson:

> A good drone pilot must know not only how to fly, but also when NOT to fly.

Throughout the training, I operated and trained using a small rotorcraft agricultural drone with payload systems.
